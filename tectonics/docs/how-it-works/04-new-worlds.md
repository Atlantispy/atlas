# From a seed to a saved tectonic world

[Guide contents](../HOW_TECTONICS_IS_MADE.md) ·
[Foundations](01-foundations.md) · [Physical processes](02-tectonic-processes.md) ·
[Coupling and reliability](03-coupling-and-reliability.md)

This chapter describes the initial-world adapters in `tectonics/tools/` and their
connections to the native calculations. They are not another W-package and are
not interchangeable with W12's fixed column demonstration. A random starting
world provides initial conditions; supported regional evolution then calculates
changes to those conditions. It does not yet simulate an entire planet's history.

The sequence is **request → candidate plates and starting crust → compatible
initial motion → saved world → admitted regional evolution → saved results**.
Crust and plate layout are prepared separately but both constrain the motion fit.
Saving, inspection and assessment act across this sequence, not just at its end.

## 1. Make a reproducible request

**Purpose and data.** A request supplies a 128-bit seed, planet radius in metres,
gravity in m/s², plate count, continental-crust fraction, frame, epoch and finite
execution limits. Fixed settings remain fixed. Auto settings draw within declared
bounds. The output is a resolved *plan*, not geometry or a finished world.

The seed is stored as text so a browser cannot round away some of its bits. A new
world can obtain fresh operating-system randomness; loading a world must retain
its existing seed. Named deterministic streams separate draws for plate sizes,
layout, crust, temperature and motion. Adding a draw for one purpose therefore
does not accidentally shuffle all the others. This is organisational independence:
changing crust still invalidates motion that depends on that crust.

The scientific identity binds the physical settings and sampling support. A
separate complete identity also records implementation/runtime and execution
configuration. Giving a calculation more memory does not itself make a different
physical world, but changing a physical input does.

**Basis and checks.** This is software design, not a geological law. Python's
[secrets](https://docs.python.org/3.13/library/secrets.html) and
[hashlib](https://docs.python.org/3.13/library/hashlib.html) provide randomness and
digest primitives; Atlas defines the versioned stream and identity contracts.
The [contract tests](../../tests/test_new_world_contract.py) include known-answer
stream checks, Fixed/Auto behaviour, replay, changed-input identities and malformed
requests. The [CLI tests](../../tests/test_new_world_cli.py) exercise persistence
and refusal. They do not establish that the reference ranges describe plausible
planets. See [the implementation](../../tools/new_world_contract.py) and
[request contract](../NEW_WORLD_CONTRACT.md).

## 2. Choose plate sizes and construct connected regions

**Purpose and data.** Radius, plate count, seed and a spherical support mesh become
an atlas of plate-owned patches, shared boundaries, areas and adjacency. Support
cells help construct the geometry; they are neither tectonic plates nor pixels.

Atlas begins with ranked areas from the exposed part of the PB2002 reference,
perturbs the weights, then normalises them to the sphere. The default multiplier
is `exp(delta)`, with `delta` drawn from `[-0.35, 0.35)`. That width is an Atlas
engineering choice, not an uncertainty measured from Earth. Different seeds can
change relative plate sizes as well as rotate or relocate the outlines.

A recursive cutting procedure groups neighbouring support cells. Sparse
shortest-path distances help choose cuts, while checks require both resulting
groups to stay connected. Sharing actual edges prevents neighbouring plates
from defining contradictory copies of their boundary. Area/resolution failures
remain failures: the generator does not secretly remove a difficult small plate
or search seeds until it finds an attractive answer.

**Basis and checks.** [Bird (2003), PB2002](https://doi.org/10.1029/2001GC000252)
provides an Earth reference, not a recipe for creating every planet. The retained
implementation uses [SciPy's sparse Dijkstra routine](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.sparse.csgraph.dijkstra.html)
for graph distances. Neither source makes Atlas's chosen partition physically
generated by mantle forces. [Layout tests](../../tests/test_new_world_layout.py)
and [morphology tests](../../tests/test_new_world_layout_morphology.py) distinguish
coverage/connectivity, rotation invariance, seed replay and area variation from
geological realism. Code: [layout adapter](../../tools/new_world_layout.py);
details and reference-use restrictions: [candidate layouts](../NEW_WORLD_LAYOUT.md).

## 3. Supply crust, materials and inherited structure

**Purpose and data.** Continental-crust fraction and seeded scenario choices become
geological regions containing layered material columns, thicknesses, formation
dates and inherited traces. Continental crust does not mean dry land: that also
depends on elevation and water level.

The current recipe puts three irregular provinces of the minority crust type
inside a whole-sphere background of the other type. Their spherical areas honour
the requested fraction. Each province shares a layered column; a seeded rotation
avoids making the geographic pole special. This is an explicit initial-condition
template. It is also the source of a known limitation: three provinces are too
restrictive to represent the desired diversity of continental arrangements.

The declared scenario uses continental crust 30–45 km thick, oceanic crust
6.2–8.0 km thick and identified reference materials. These are scenario values,
not measurements of a generated planet. An inherited trace can have a location
and width while its strength stays unknown. A line on a map does not manufacture
active slip, accumulated damage or a subducting slab.

**Basis and checks.** [Fraters et al. (2019), Geodynamic World Builder](https://se.copernicus.org/articles/10/1785/2019/)
supports describing geological features independently of the numerical sampling
grid. Atlas implements its own precursor and uses its native sampler; it does
not execute World Builder here. [Structure tests](../../tests/test_new_world_structure.py)
check requested spherical coverage, per-material mixed-cell accounts, reproducible
variation and independence from plate count/support resolution. Those checks
establish consistent construction, not realistic continental morphology. Code:
[starting structure](../../tools/new_world_structure.py); assumptions and material
references: [initial lithosphere](../NEW_WORLD_STRUCTURE.md).

## 4. Assign initial temperature without inventing thermal history

**Purpose and data.** Layer thicknesses, conductivity, heat production, boundary
temperatures and an explicitly selected thermal age produce depth/temperature
profiles in metres and kelvin.

Continental columns use a steady layered conduction model. Heat flux and temperature
match at interfaces; internal heat production affects the profile. Oceanic columns
reuse the W03 finite-plate cooling solution with a selected cooling age. A crustal
formation date and a cooling date coincide only under the stated no-reheating
assumption. Continental formation age is not silently used as a cooling clock.

Profiles are saved as shared compact tables. Their interpolation error is bounded
against the underlying analytical profile, and the sampler integrates them over
actual radial volume. A small interpolation error measures numerical representation,
not error in the chosen physical assumptions.

**Basis and checks.** The [thermal foundations chapter](01-foundations.md) explains
the W03 cooling/conduction basis. [Thermal adapter tests](../../tests/test_new_world_thermal.py)
compare with independent quadratic/Fourier solutions, test interface heat balance,
cooling with age and refusal when a profile cannot meet its bound. Code:
[thermal tables](../../tools/new_world_thermal.py).

The present ocean-majority recipe gives its oceanic background one cooling age.
That is not a spatially varying seafloor-age reconstruction. Making a convincing
age map requires an explicit spreading history; distance to the nearest current
ridge is not a general substitute. This remains a realism gap, not a hidden
capability of the existing W06 regional history solver.

## 5. Fit initial plate motions to the crust and junctions

**Purpose and data.** Plate geometry, shared geological spans and seeded proposed
rotations produce an Euler vector for each plate, local boundary velocities and
junction diagnostics. An Euler vector specifies a rotation axis and angular speed
in rad/s; surface velocity in m/s follows from `velocity = omega × position`.

The small constrained fit balances three considerations. It stays near the
proposed rotations, favours less relative motion across shared continental
material, and requires a single junction velocity compatible with its incident
boundaries. The continental-affinity coefficient is currently 4: a dimensionless
scenario preference, not measured rock strength. Rank ambiguity or the loss of
all relative motion causes a refusal.

The fit removes the arbitrary shared rotation before choosing a display anchor.
Choosing a different stationary-looking plate must not change what neighbouring
plates do relative to each other. This is not a physical no-net-rotation reference
frame. Finally, boundary-length-weighted relative speed is scaled to a seeded
1–8 cm/year target. Matching that imposed target is a scaling check, not an
independent prediction of plate speed.

**Basis and checks.** [pyGPlates velocity conventions](https://www.gplates.org/docs/pygplates/generated/pygplates.calculate_velocities)
provide a reference for rotation-based velocities; pyGPlates is not run by this
adapter. Equality-constrained fitting is explained in
[Boyd and Vandenberghe, Convex Optimization](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/).
Atlas's particular geological preference remains its own uncalibrated recipe.
[Frame tests](../../tests/test_new_world_motion_frame.py) compare an independent
augmented least-squares formulation and changes of anchor. The recorded correction
removes anchor-dependent relative motion, but its whole integration receipt is
now classified historical. Code: [motion fit](../../tools/new_world_motion.py);
method and evidence scope: [initial motion](../NEW_WORLD_MOTION.md).

## 6. Interpret complete boundaries, not just two plate arrows

The velocity difference is evaluated at the same position on both sides of a
shared edge. It is separated into opening/shortening normal to the edge and sliding
along it. An oblique boundary can have both. Exact geological intersections and
normal-velocity zero crossings divide edges into meaningful spans; one midpoint
cannot reliably label the whole edge. Numerically unresolved tiny normal motion
stays unresolved rather than being promoted to a geological fault type.

The initial boundary law uses the average normal speed of the adjacent plates.
Its junction constraints describe compatibility at the initial instant. They do
not prove that the network can keep evolving without changing its topology.
[McKenzie and Morgan (1969), Evolution of Triple Junctions](https://www.nature.com/articles/224125a0)
is the relevant conceptual reference; this documentation uses its abstract-level
scope, not a claim that Atlas reproduces the full paper's stability analysis.

Outputs preserve normal and shear components, contacts, junction velocities and
age references. Opening at this initial epoch means *incipient* extension: no
new crust has yet been formed by that event. A converging span does not by itself
specify slab polarity or justify switching on subduction.

Checks include analytic arc integrals, normal sign changes, side reversal,
geological intersections and junction residuals in
[motion tests](../../tests/test_new_world_motion.py),
[arc tests](../../tests/test_new_world_arcs.py) and
[motion assessment](../../tests/test_assess_new_world_motion.py). Code:
[arc geometry](../../tools/new_world_arcs.py) and
[read-only motion assessment](../../tools/assess_new_world_motion.py).

## 7. Admit a regional experiment from the saved world

**Purpose and data.** A selected boundary, footprint, forcing duration and saved
world supply a local computational region with actual sampled materials and
motion. The global world file remains unchanged.

Admission checks the whole footprint, not just its centre: it must fit the
supported geological column and selected plate pair without crossing an
unhandled boundary. The spherical-to-local chart has stated distortion and
velocity-variation bounds. Unsupported requests are refused, not silently shrunk.
Native W01 sampling preserves spherical shell volumes; those volumes are converted
to equivalent planar thickness rather than treating spherical area × radial depth
as the same quantity without correction.

**Checks and connection.** The [native-input tests](../../tests/test_new_world_native.py)
cover admission, sampled inventories, unknown-value masks and source changes.
[W01 sampling](01-foundations.md) supplies the scientific method; this adapter adds
the rules for its use on a generated world. Code:
[native input assembly](../../tools/new_world_native.py).

## 8. Calculate deformation and its vertical response

The local velocity gradient retains both across-boundary and along-boundary
motion. W08's finite affine map evolves material parcels; their reference volumes
remain conserved. Smaller horizontal area makes a fixed volume thicker, and
larger area makes it thinner. Shear changes shape but must not create area loss
or uplift by itself. Common translation is retained too.

The connected vertical response is explicitly **dry local Airy compensation**:
columns balance independently above a denser mantle, with no elastic bending.
For each layer's thickness change `dH`, its contribution to upward surface change
is `(1 - layer_density / mantle_density) × dH`. The corresponding base movement
closes the column-weight balance. This is one selected support model, not an
extra uplift term to add on top of W04 flexure.

The outputs include evolved footprints, thickness and reference-relative surface
and base changes. They are not absolute elevation, a coastline or a globally
evolved thermal field. Initial temperature retains its initial-state provenance;
unknown enthalpy is not replaced with zero. This route prescribes distributed
deformation, rather than calculating localisation from regional stress.

**Basis and checks.** [Physical-process methods](02-tectonic-processes.md) cover
the finite deformation; [support methods](01-foundations.md) explain local versus
elastic response and their sources. Independent area/thickness and column-weight
controls, pure shear/translation zero-relief cases and constant inventories are
in [evolution tests](../../tests/test_new_world_evolution.py). Code:
[regional evolution](../../tools/new_world_evolution.py); exact assumptions:
[generated regional evolution](../NEW_WORLD_EVOLUTION.md).

## 9. Save the world and resume only compatible work

An initial `.atlas` project preserves the actual plan, atlas and available
geological/motion records. It restores these values rather than rerunning the
seed. Regional jobs save immutable requests, initial data and committed output
prefixes so a supported interruption can resume without recalculating completed
outputs. An incomplete preparation is not treated as a restartable checkpoint.

A portable bundle can contain one original world and selected regional jobs.
Already compressed world data is stored once, not recompressed for every job.
Strict member and size checks, hashes and exclusive publication protect integrity.
Hashes identify bytes; they are not a digital signature or scientific approval.

Read-only access and continuation have different admission rules. An otherwise
supported historical job can be inspected with its original identity retained,
while continuation is refused if the current execution binding differs. Native
world decoding still has to work; this is not a promise of arbitrary cross-platform
restart or permission to replace stored hashes.

**Basis and checks.** This reuses Atlas's lossless storage and transaction methods
in [the reliability chapter](03-coupling-and-reliability.md), not a new geology
model. See [project code](../../tools/new_world_project.py),
[job code](../../tools/new_world_job.py), [bundle code](../../tools/new_world_bundle.py),
and their [project](../../tests/test_new_world_project.py),
[job](../../tests/test_new_world_job.py), [bundle](../../tests/test_new_world_bundle.py)
and [session](../../tests/test_new_world_session.py) tests. The
[bundle contract](../NEW_WORLD_BUNDLE.md) defines exact refusal/recovery behaviour.

## 10. Assess an actual generated world

Three different questions need different checks:

- **Valid geometry:** does the sphere have complete, non-overlapping ownership,
  connected plates, consistent shared edges and no longitude-seam artefact?
- **Physical and numerical consistency:** do sampled inventories, finite area
  changes, thickness and column-weight balance agree with independent controls?
- **Realistic morphology and kinematics:** do boundary turning, shapes, sizes,
  neighbours and motion alignment behave plausibly at matched observation scales?

The [combined acceptance tool](../../tools/check_new_world_acceptance.py) exercises
fixed cases through creation, saving, reopening, evolution and fresh-process
continuation. The [r2 record](../../evidence/new-world-s7-r2.json) reports all nine
phases passing for its three-case repair snapshot. The native source inventory
then changed: the [current-evidence register](../../../docs/CURRENT_EVIDENCE.md)
correctly treats that numerical receipt as historical. Its current case matrix
is a test definition, not a current passing run. No simulations were rerun to
write this guide.

Visual inspection must use the actual saved geometry/arrays with a stated legend
and frame. It can expose grid zig-zags, implausibly uniform continents or a flat
ocean-age field; it cannot establish conservation or prove empirical accuracy.
An illustrative terrain image is not a rendered tectonics result.

The [morphology assessment](../../tools/assess_plate_layout_morphology.py) keeps
reference exclusions, observation scale and withheld samples explicit. The
[boundary coalescing/fairing helper](../../tools/new_world_boundary.py) remains an
unintegrated diagnostic prototype, not the chosen realism repair. B1's matched
measurement is implemented; the [causal-generation diagnosis](../NEW_WORLD_LAYOUT.md#causal-generation-diagnosis-and-replacement-design)
shows that the missing evolution layer links the boundary, starting-crust and
ocean-age defects. The replacement plan starts with coherent evolving state and
history, then a causal boundary producer, not post-generation smoothing. See
[Phase B](../../../docs/ATLAS_ROADMAP.md#5-phase-b--finish-tectonics-realism-and-the-usable-tectonics-module).
